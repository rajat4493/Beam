# Beam value hypothesis: paid participation, not tips

**Status:** product thesis, not yet validated with external creators or fans.

## The comparison a creator and fan will make

| Choice | What the payment reliably does | What it does not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creator’s UPI QR | Sends money directly to the creator | Creates no shared live moment, priority, status, or verified on-screen interaction |
| YouTube Super Chat | Keeps the viewer in YouTube; highlights/pins a chat message; supports reactions, milestones, and creator reporting | Lets the creator define only the interaction YouTube supports |
| Beam | Direct creator-owned payment plus a verified, durable, live OBS interaction | Cannot promise zero context switching on mobile |

## Non-negotiable positioning

Do not call the purchase a “donation” or lead with “lower fees.”

> **Beam sells paid participation:** support unlocks a creator-defined live action that the whole audience can see.

The money is necessary, but not the product. A static QR plus an OBS thank-you card is not enough to replace Super Chat or justify a mobile handoff.

## What YouTube already does well

YouTube describes Supers as highlighted/pinned messages or animated stickers. It also provides purchase milestones, public profile/name/amount display, replies/hearts/likes, live-control-room visibility, historical purchase views, and revenue reporting. The creator receives 70% of confirmed Supers revenue under YouTube's stated model. These are meaningful baseline expectations, not optional extras.

## Hypotheses worth testing before building

Test one interaction class at a time with a creator who explicitly agrees to honor it live:

1. **Priority question:** a paid question enters the creator’s visible answer queue.
2. **Challenge:** the creator can accept or decline a paid audience challenge.
3. **Poll power:** a contribution visibly advances a creator-defined poll or goal.
4. **Hype moment:** a contribution triggers a stream-specific celebration the community recognizes.

Each needs a clear fan outcome within the stream. “Your name appeared briefly” is a weak hypothesis; “the creator answered my question” is testable.

## Explicitly rejected hypotheses

- A lower payment fee by itself wins.
- UPI receive limits are the wedge. Limits and bank/PSP behavior vary; no evidence supports making this the business thesis.
- An embedded player solves mobile friction.
- Features such as badges, AI, or themes solve abandonment caused by leaving YouTube.

## Measurement for the first creator experiments

Capture a **decision**, not merely a payment:

- Stream/viewer context and interaction offered
- Supporter saw the Beam option
- Supporter opened Beam / reached Checkout / completed payment / returned to YouTube
- Creator actually honored the interaction
- Supporter’s stated reason for choosing or declining Beam
- Repeat use on a later stream

Do not interpret an adoption percentage until there are at least 30–50 paid-support decisions across streams. The early strongest signal is: the creator says they will use Beam next stream, and supporters return voluntarily.

## Decision gate

Proceed only if a defined interaction earns the context switch. If viewers repeatedly reach checkout but abandon because it interrupts viewing, redesign the interaction or stop; do not mask that signal with more surface features.
